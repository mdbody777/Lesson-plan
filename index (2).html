Lesson Plan: Schistosoma mansoni

Faculty of Veterinary Medicine

1. Lesson Details

Teacher Dina Mahmoud School/Faculty Faculty of Veterinary Medicine
Subject & Grade Parasitology, 3rd Year Date
Duration & Format 50 minutes, Face-to-Face Class Profile 60 students, mixed ability
Previous Learning Ascaris lumbricoides

2. Learning Objectives

By the end of the 50-minute lesson, learners will be able to:

RememberIdentify the definitive and intermediate hosts of Schistosoma mansoni, listing at least two characteristic morphological features.
UnderstandExplain the life cycle of Schistosoma mansoni, detailing the stages from egg to adult worm in a sequential diagram.
ApplyCorrelate the pathophysiological changes in the host (e.g., hepatic fibrosis) with specific stages of the parasite's life cycle.
AnalyseCompare and contrast the infection pathway of Schistosoma mansoni with that of Ascaris lumbricoides (studied previously), identifying three key differences.
EvaluateAssess the effectiveness of current preventative measures (e.g., molluscicides vs. sanitation) in breaking the transmission cycle in endemic areas.
CreateDesign a rapid community awareness poster outlining the transmission route and three primary prevention strategies for schistosomiasis.

3. Lesson Procedures

Stage Student Actions Cooperative Structure Differentiation Time
Warm-up
DOK 1		 Learners recall the life cycle of Ascaris lumbricoides from the previous lesson. They brainstorm the concept of a "parasite life cycle" and list the types of hosts required for completion. Think-Pair-Share: Think individually about Ascaris, pair up to discuss the concept of hosts, share key takeaways with the class. Sup: Provide a word bank of host types.
Cor: Identify definitive vs. intermediate hosts.
Ext: Suggest why some parasites need multiple hosts.		 4m
Key Terms
DOK 1		 Learners encounter specific terminology: cercariae, miracidium, Biomphalaria (snail intermediate host), and hepatic portal system. They match terms to definitions on a slide. Numbered Heads: Groups of 4. Each student gets a number. Teacher calls a number, and that student answers for the group. Sup: Use visual aids for each term.
Cor: Match term to definition.
Ext: Write a sentence using the new terminology.		 6m
First Encounter
DOK 2		 Learners observe a diagram of the Schistosoma mansoni life cycle. They identify the definitive host (human) and the intermediate host (snail) and the environments involved (freshwater). Placemat: Groups of 4 sketch the life cycle on a shared large sheet, taking turns to add the next stage. Sup: Provide a partially completed diagram.
Cor: Complete the full life cycle diagram.
Ext: Predict the most vulnerable point in the life cycle for intervention.		 6m
Guided Practice
DOK 2		 Learners trace the path of the cercariae penetrating the skin and migrating to the mesenteric veins. They label a provided anatomical diagram of the human host indicating the infection site. Rally Coach: Pairs take turns explaining the migration path to each other. Partner A speaks, Partner B checks accuracy, then switch. Sup: Provide a step-by-step flowchart for the migration path.
Cor: Trace and label the migration path on the diagram.
Ext: Hypothesize the physiological reason the worms choose the mesenteric veins.		 10m
Deep Analysis
DOK 3		 Learners analyze the pathology. They connect the eggs trapping in the liver to the development of hepatic fibrosis and portal hypertension. They contrast this skin penetration method with the ingestion method of Ascaris. Jigsaw: Expert groups analyze either liver pathology, intestinal pathology, or transmission comparison. They then return to home groups to teach their findings. Sup: Provide guiding questions for the pathology analysis.
Cor: Explain the link between eggs and fibrosis.
Ext: Analyze how the differing entry methods (skin vs. ingestion) affect the initial immune response.		 9m
Application
DOK 3		 Learners review a case study of a patient presenting with symptoms of schistosomiasis. They must identify the likely exposure event based on the life cycle and suggest diagnostic methods. Fishbowl: A small group discusses the case study in the center while the rest of the class observes and takes notes, then swaps. Sup: Highlight key symptoms in the case study text.
Cor: Identify exposure and suggest a diagnostic test (e.g., stool exam).
Ext: Evaluate the limitations of relying solely on stool exams for chronic cases.		 7m
Synthesis
DOK 4		 Learners evaluate preventative strategies. They debate the efficacy of mass drug administration (praziquantel) versus snail control and improved sanitation infrastructure. Four Corners: Students move to corners representing different primary prevention strategies and defend their choice to their peers. Sup: Provide a pros/cons list for each strategy.
Cor: Argue for the most effective strategy based on the life cycle.
Ext: Propose a multi-pronged public health intervention plan.		 4m
Close
DOK 4		 Learners draft a rapid community awareness message. They outline the key transmission route and three vital prevention tips. They complete an exit ticket summarizing the main differences between Ascaris and Schistosoma transmission. Gallery Walk: Students display their awareness messages for peer review before submitting their exit tickets. Sup: Use a template for the awareness message.
Cor: Draft the message and complete the exit ticket.
Ext: Design the message for a specific high-risk demographic (e.g., fishermen).		 4m

4. Assessment & Rubric

Success Criteria: Accurate identification of hosts, logical sequencing of the life cycle, correct correlation of pathology to parasite stage, and a clear, medically accurate awareness message.

Criteria 4 Marks (Excellent) 3 Marks (Good) 2 Marks (Developing) 1 Mark (Needs Support)
Life Cycle Knowledge Flawless detail of all stages and hosts. Correct stages and hosts; minor omissions. Basic understanding; some stages confused. Significant errors in life cycle sequence.
Pathology Correlation Accurately links egg deposition to fibrosis. Explains pathology with minor inaccuracies. Struggles to connect parasite stage to disease. Cannot correlate parasite to pathology.
Comparative Analysis Insightful comparison with Ascaris transmission. Adequate comparison; identifies main differences. Superficial comparison; misses key differences. Cannot distinguish transmission methods.
Awareness Message Clear, accurate, and highly persuasive design. Accurate information; adequately presented. Information present but lacks clarity/impact. Inaccurate information or confusing design.

Bands: 14–16 Exceeding · 11–13 Meeting · 7–10 Developing · 4–6 Needs support.
